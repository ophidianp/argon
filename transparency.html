Transparency

How Argon was made.

Straight answers about where the idea came from, who wrote the code, and what happens to your data.

The idea

Inspired by Symvibe

Argon started from the idea behind Symvibe, a Mac app for running coding agents on a shared board. Argon is its own app for Windows, written from scratch, with its own name, look and code.

The code

Built with AI

Argon and this website were written with Claude, an AI model, directed and tested by Pavan. The design follows the same style as Chlorine and the other sites in this family.

Your data

Stays on your PC

Argon has no servers, accounts or telemetry. Boards, notes, memory and settings are JSON files in your app data folder, with rolling backups.

Your accounts

Never touched

Agents sign in by themselves. Argon only checks whether each agent has saved a login file, to show "signed in" in the setup panel. It never reads or sends what's inside.

Usage stats

Read locally

The usage chart reads Claude Code and Codex session logs already on your PC and adds up token counts. Nothing leaves your machine.

Safety

Approvals stay on

Agents ask before editing files or running commands. Auto-approve is off by default and comes with a warning if you turn it on.

Questions?

Have a look for yourself. It's all local.

Run Argon from source and read every line, or open your data folder from Settings.
